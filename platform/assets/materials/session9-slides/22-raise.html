🚦 raise — throw your own errors
So far you caught errors. Now learn to throw them on purpose.
Why raise at all? 🤔
▶ Your function gets impossible data.
▶ You refuse to compute garbage.
▶ You signal "caller, you made a mistake" — loudly.

🧠 Think of raise as quality control at the gate: bad input never gets past you.
The raised exception can still be caught with try/except by the caller — raising ≠ crashing.
raise.py
def set_age(age):
    if age < 0:
        raise ValueError("Age can't be negative")
    print(f("Age set to {age}"))
 
set_age(17)  # ✅ prints normally
set_age(-5)  # 💥 raises ValueError
The message you pass to ValueError(...) becomes the visible diagnosis. Make it clear and specific.
